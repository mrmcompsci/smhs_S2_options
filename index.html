Computing Science

In S3 Computing Science you learn to solve problems by breaking them into steps a computer can follow. You will write programs in Python, build your own websites with HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and find out what goes on inside the computers you use every day. If you enjoy it, you can carry on to National 5 in S4.

Computing Science

What will you learn?

  • Use computational thinking to break large problems into smaller steps
  • How computer systems in general operate, their main components and how they work together
  • How to analyse, design, build, test and evaluate software and website projects
  • How to code (write software) using the Python programming language
  • How to build websites using HTML, CSS and JavaScript
  • How to work as part of a team

NPA Digital Passport (SCQF 4)

The NPA Digital Passport gives you a nationally recognised Computing qualification built from short, practical units instead of one big exam at the end. In S3 you will study three units: Computer Programming or Data Security, Social Media Literacy, and Web Apps: Presentations. In S4 you can move on to the Digital Passport at SCQF 5.

  • The history of social media, and how to use it safely and responsibly

Graphic Communication

Graphic Communication teaches you to draw and design the way engineers, architects and product designers do. Over two years you will sketch by hand, build and render 3D models in CAD, produce accurate technical drawings and design your own promotional layouts. It suits anyone who likes making an idea look clear and convincing, on paper or on screen.

Practical Woodworking

Practical Woodworking is spent almost entirely in the workshop. Over two years you will learn to use hand tools, power tools and the woodturning lathe to build frames and cabinets, cut joints to within a millimetre and give your work a proper finish. At National 5 there is no exam: your grade comes from the piece you make, a short case study and your workshop log book.

What will you learn?

  • Measuring, marking out and cutting timber accurately using hand and power tools
  • Constructing traditional and modern joints that hold under real use
  • Selecting and applying finishes to protect and present a finished piece
  • Reading a working drawing and turning it into a physical object, start to finish

Practical Metalworking

Practical Metalworking takes you from filing and drilling at the bench to turning parts on a centre lathe, forging hot steel and welding. You will study three units: Bench Skills, Machine Processes, and Fabrication and Thermal Joining. You will work to drawings accurate to half a millimetre and learn the safe working practices every workshop depends on. At National 5 there is no exam: your grade comes from the product you make, a short case study and your workshop log book.

Pathway
CfE Level 4
→
National 5
Practical Metalworking
→
NPA Built Environment within a Global InfrastructureSCQF 6

What will you learn?

  • Use metalworking hand tools to measure, mark out and cut/shape metal
  • Machining processes on the Centre Lathe and Pillar Drill
  • Working with a range of materials
  • Joining techniques including welding, brazing and mechanical fastening
  • Safe working practices in the workshop
  • Reading a technical drawing and turning it into an accurate, working piece

  • You like the idea of building something that has to actually work, not just look right

Creative Thinking

Creative Thinking is built around real challenges set by companies and organisations. On each project you research the problem, come up with ideas, test them, fix what fails and present your best solution. You build a portfolio of your work as you go, and you can show your thinking through sketching, photography, film, music or whatever suits you.
